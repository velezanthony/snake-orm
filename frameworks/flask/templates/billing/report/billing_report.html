Billing report

The roll call of the tariffs, the money they actually brought in, and the gap between the two. Three statements, none of which grows with the number of invoices. Money is stored here as integer cents and turned into something readable through Decimal, never through a float — that conversion lives in exactly one function in the repository.

Every plan, and who is on it

annotate() — a COUNT of the subscriptions riding in the same statement as the plan. A plan nobody subscribes to comes back with a zero, which is the row a GROUP BY over the subscriptions would silently drop and the one a "which tariffs are dead" question is about.

The oldest debts still open

snake_date_add(Invoice.issued_at, 30, DAY) — the due date is computed by the ENGINE, so it can be projected, compared and ordered by without a single row leaving the database. Thirty DAYS and not one month: "net 30" is counted in days, and days are the unit the three engines agree on — SQLite overflows a calendar month.

snake_cast(collected, float) / snake_cast(amount, float) — both columns are integer cents, so without the cast this is integer division and every partly-paid invoice reads 0%. The collected figure is a correlated SUM over that invoice's payments, in the SAME statement: asking per row would be an N+1.
